# What Was Missing — Product Critique V02

## Critical omissions in the earlier concept
1. **The work layer**: a profession is not just title + skills; it is tasks, workflows, decisions, tools and handoffs.
2. **The novice path**: recommending AI is useless if the person cannot choose, install, configure or verify it.
3. **The implementation gap**: learning, setup and done-for-you service are different delivery modes.
4. **The adoption gap**: fear, trust, language, access, confidence, time and organizational permission block use.
5. **The economics layer**: subscription price alone misses setup, review, maintenance and failure costs.
6. **The integration layer**: tools must connect to the systems where work actually happens.
7. **The governance layer**: permissions, sensitive data, audit, rollback and accountability.
8. **The quality layer**: workflows need representative tests and regression checks.
9. **The feedback layer**: measure actual outcomes, not clicks or self-reported enthusiasm.
10. **The new opportunity layer**: AI can create new services and hybrid roles, not only change existing tasks.
11. **The human support layer**: some users need a coach, specialist or implementer.
12. **The localization layer**: language, payment, regional access, local standards and low-bandwidth use.
13. **The maintenance layer**: models, connectors, prices, UIs and policies change.
14. **The exit layer**: export, vendor lock-in, credential revocation and workflow retirement.
15. **The organizational change layer**: team adoption requires ownership, communication and worker involvement.

## Further improvements recommended
- Start with one profession and one narrow, measurable workflow, not a giant directory.
- Build an evidence graph so every claim has source, date, geography and confidence.
- Separate potential, pilot evidence and validated production outcomes.
- Offer three user paths: learn it, guide me, do it for me.
- Prefer "workflow recipe" over prompt library as the core unit of value.
- Include "do not use AI here" guidance as a first-class output.
- Build a maintenance and review system from day one.
- Keep the product vendor-neutral; do not make one model/provider the whole product.
- Avoid an unverified universal ranking of AI tools.
- Treat the service marketplace as a later phase after quality standards and provider vetting exist.

## Strategic risk
The product can become too broad: career advice + AI directory + LMS + automation builder + services marketplace + job board. These are not one MVP. Keep a shared data model, but launch in stages around one verified user outcome.
