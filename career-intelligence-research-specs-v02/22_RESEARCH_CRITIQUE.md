# Research Critique — Revised

## High confidence
- Career assessment is an established category.
- Basic quizzes and AI career chat are crowded.
- Resume-to-job matching is crowded.
- O*NET/ESCO/Lightcast demonstrate the need for structured occupation/skill data.
- Geography and work context materially affect career feasibility.
- Adjacent transitions are more useful than isolated career scores.
- Central Asia already has AI career/job products, including BEEK and TalentBridge.

## Medium confidence
- A multi-signal profile can improve usefulness.
- Localized occupation/geo modules can be a wedge.
- Evidence-linked recommendations can improve trust.
- B2B2C may reduce acquisition cost.

## Low confidence
- Users will pay enough B2C.
- Better recommendations cause better employment outcomes.
- The phrase «career decision engine» is itself differentiated.
- Central Asia is the optimal first market.
- Proprietary data will create a durable moat before incumbents copy the workflow.

## Most important correction
Earlier research treated «all-in-one» as a potential differentiator. It is not. Aptly and Indeed already cover much of this workflow.

## Strategic implication
The moat must come from:
- better profile normalization;
- profession-specific modules;
- local market data;
- evidence quality;
- transition planning;
- outcome feedback;
- proprietary longitudinal data.

## Main product risk
Scope explosion. Solve with universal core + modular profession packs + geo packs.

## Main algorithmic risk
False precision. Use evidence/confidence/trade-offs before calibrated numeric probabilities.

## Main business risk
Beautiful report without action. Optimize decision → plan → proof → application → outcome.
